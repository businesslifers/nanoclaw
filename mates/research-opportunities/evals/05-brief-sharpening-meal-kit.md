# Brief sharpening for a meal-kit subscription campaign

## Task

Given the following — *"An existing creative brief for a meal-kit subscription campaign is built around the core insight 'busy parents don't have time to cook'. The brief asks for a validation pass: is this the strongest available insight for this category, or is there sharper signal in the VOC evidence? Output is a brief-validation note that either confirms the existing insight or recommends a revised one, with evidence either way."* — produce the brief-validation note.

## Conditions to compare

1. Baseline model (no mate context)
2. Mate prompt only
3. Mate prompt + wiki
4. Mate fully loaded under a real claw with prior meal-kit category VOC and competitor messaging audit available

## What good looks like

- Explicit verdict at the top of the note: supported / partially supported / contradicted, with one sentence summarising why.
- Evidence supporting the verdict drawn from observable VOC sources — community discussion threads about meal-kit subscription decisions, churn-reason reviews on review surfaces, recipe-difficulty and prep-time discussion in cooking communities, decision-framing in subscription-cancellation feedback — with source types cited.
- Honest engagement with the existing insight. "Busy parents don't have time to cook" might be partially supported (it surfaces in some VOC) but the stronger signal could be elsewhere — e.g. *I'm sick of deciding what to eat every night*, *I want to eat better but I can't sustain meal planning*, *I want my kids to eat what I cook without a meltdown*, *I cancelled because the food was fine but the packaging waste was unconscionable*. The note doesn't dismiss the original insight; it weighs it against alternatives.
- At least one alternative angle named with its own VOC evidence, including why that angle has stronger or different deployment potential than the existing insight.
- Clear recommendation on whether to proceed with the existing brief, sharpen it, or revise it materially — and what specifically to change if sharpening or revising.
- Buyer-language extract appended with phrases from each angle considered, so the receiving creative team can compare voices directly.
- Confidence calibration honest — the recommendation does not overclaim certainty where the VOC signal is mixed.

## What bad looks like

- Validation note simply ratifies the existing insight without testing it against VOC evidence
- Validation note dismisses the existing insight wholesale without weighing it honestly
- Alternative angles named but without evidence — opinions presented as VOC findings
- Buyer-language extract missing, or composed of paraphrased marketing voice
- Recommendation is "either could work" — no actual call made
- VOC evidence cited is generic ("people say meal kits are convenient") rather than specific, source-tagged, audience-language quotes
- No acknowledgement of category-specific dynamics — e.g. churn signal in meal-kit reviews tends to be more diagnostic than acquisition signal, because the audience is well past the decision point and articulating what actually broke

## Notes

This eval tests brief-sharpening discipline against a plausible-sounding but possibly under-sharp insight. The failure mode in both directions: rubber-stamping the brief because it sounds reasonable, or rejecting it for the sake of having something to say. A strong answer engages with the existing insight on the VOC evidence and is willing to land on partial support, full support, or material revision depending on what the signal actually shows.
