# VOC sourcing plan for a B2B SaaS targeting small-business accountants

## Task

Given the following brief — *"A B2B SaaS product is launching into the small-business-accountant segment in Australia. Before any opportunity work is commissioned, the brief asks for a sourcing plan: explain how you would find voice-of-customer signal for this audience — which platforms and communities to research, what to search for, what good signal looks like in this category, and what specifically would be different from a consumer-VOC approach."* — produce the VOC sourcing plan.

## Conditions to compare

1. Baseline model (no mate context)
2. Mate prompt only
3. Mate prompt + wiki
4. Mate fully loaded under a real claw with prior B2B sourcing plans available

## What good looks like

- Audience type named explicitly, with the implications for VOC sourcing called out — B2B accountants describe their working life and tool frustrations on professional-network surfaces, industry-specific review platforms (software-comparison surfaces aimed at business buyers), and trade or industry forums; their VOC signal is not heaviest on open consumer community platforms.
- Primary VOC surfaces named with specific rationale — not just "look on Reddit" but which professional or industry-specific subreddits or community spaces carry small-business-accountant signal in Australia, which software-review surfaces are calibrated to business buyers, which industry associations or trade publications host community discussion.
- At least one non-obvious source surfaced (e.g. accountant-specific community spaces, podcast comment threads, industry conference back-channels, trade-publication letter-page or comment sections, professional-network discussion groups) — the answer demonstrates awareness that B2B signal is rarely strongest on the same surfaces as consumer signal.
- Specific search and reading patterns named — what queries surface frustration signal, what review-platform filters surface decision-driving feedback, what dates and contexts (end-of-financial-year, software-rollover, regulatory-change moments) generate stronger signal in this category.
- What good vs. weak signal looks like is calibrated for the category — a strongly-worded one-paragraph review of a competing tool from a working accountant is high-signal; a vendor-marketing-style "comparison" post on a low-quality affiliate site is low-signal; sustained discussion in a closed professional group beats a single one-line post on a generic forum.
- Calibration for the Australian market — naming where Australian-specific signal lives (state-based industry associations, ATO and regulatory-context discussion, AU-specific accounting software conversation) versus where US or UK signal would mislead.

## What bad looks like

- Plan reads as a generic consumer-VOC plan with "B2B" pasted on the title — same platforms, same queries, no acknowledgement that the audience does not heavily populate those spaces
- Only one platform type named (e.g. "search Reddit") with no recognition that B2B VOC requires multiple platform types
- No non-obvious source named; everything in the plan would be obvious to anyone who has done one piece of VOC research before
- "What good signal looks like" treated as universal rather than calibrated for the category
- No acknowledgement of the difference between Australian and US/UK accountant discourse
- Sourcing plan recommends paid research (panels, surveys) rather than the publicly-available VOC the role specifically uses
- Plan is structured as a list of tools rather than a research strategy

## Notes

This eval tests range against an audience type where the default consumer-VOC playbook underperforms. The failure mode: a competent-looking sourcing plan that would lead a junior researcher to spend a week on the wrong platforms and come back empty-handed. A strong answer treats B2B sourcing as a different research problem, not a flavoured version of the consumer one.
