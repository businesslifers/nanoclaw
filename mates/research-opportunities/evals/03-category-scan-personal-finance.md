# Category scan for personal finance, late-30s pre-retirement

## Task

Given the following brief — *"Produce an opportunity brief for the personal-finance category in Australia, targeting people in their late thirties who are starting to think about retirement planning for the first time. No specific brand is named — output should be usable by any provider in the category (super funds, financial advisers, retail-banking wealth arms, financial-literacy publishers) to brief content and advertising work."* — produce the opportunity brief.

## Conditions to compare

1. Baseline model (no mate context)
2. Mate prompt only
3. Mate prompt + wiki
4. Mate fully loaded under a real claw with category VOC source maps for personal finance available

## What good looks like

- Three to five opportunities in the standard five-part format.
- Opportunities reflect real anxiety and confusion language found in VOC sources (community forums, review surfaces, video comments, search-intent surfaces) — not generic "people want financial security" framings.
- Specific buying-moment signal — e.g. someone who's just had a child and is calculating super contributions for the first time, someone who realised mid-career that the default super fund is underperforming, someone trying to consolidate accounts and getting paralysed by the comparison work — not category-level abstraction.
- Gap clearly named per opportunity. The personal-finance category is content-saturated overall; opportunities have to point at the specific corners where the content is missing, wrong, or condescending rather than just "people are interested in retirement planning".
- Confidence differentiated across the set; high-confidence opportunities are anchored in multiple-source signal plus rising search demand, and lower-confidence ones explicitly flagged.
- Deployment paths suit the signal — long-form explainers for high-confusion decisions, short-form video for myth-busting against widely-believed misinformation, calculator or interactive content where the audience signal is "I can't model this for myself".
- Buyer-language extract organised by emotional theme — *I left it too late*, *I don't know who to trust*, *I'm scared I'm going to make it worse*, etc. — with five to eight tagged phrases.
- Regulatory environment named where it shapes what can be said in this category in Australia.

## What bad looks like

- Opportunities frame the audience in category language ("emerging mid-career investors are uncertain about retirement planning") rather than real-person language
- Opportunities sit at the abstract level of "people want financial security" rather than naming specific decision moments
- Gap not named — opportunities surface signal in a content-saturated category without explaining why this signal is under-served
- All opportunities marked high-confidence
- Buyer-language extract written in marketing voice — paraphrased into "smooth" copy rather than preserving the actual messiness of how people talk about money anxiety
- No reference to the regulatory environment that bounds claims and product positioning in this category
- US-based personal-finance content treated as Australian signal without flagging the difference in tax, super, and regulatory framing

## Notes

This eval tests category-scan discipline in a content-saturated space. The failure mode: producing opportunities that look fine in isolation but every one of which is already covered by ten existing pieces of content. A strong answer earns its opportunities by demonstrating they sit in the actual content gaps, not just on the surface of an interesting category.
