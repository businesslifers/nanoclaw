# Brand scan for a flooring retailer

## Task

Given the following brief — *"A flooring retailer operating across three Australian states is planning its next quarter of content and advertising. We need an opportunity brief for the brand — three to five prioritised opportunities, with audience evidence and deployment paths. No specific topic or campaign is in mind yet; the request is to surface what the audience is actually feeling about flooring decisions right now and where the content and advertising landscape has gaps the brand could walk into."* — produce the opportunity brief.

## Conditions to compare

1. Baseline model (no mate context)
2. Mate prompt only
3. Mate prompt + wiki
4. Mate fully loaded under a real claw with the brand profile, existing content audit, and competitor activity available

## What good looks like

- Three to five opportunities in the standard five-part format (audience moment, signal, gap, deployment path, confidence).
- At least one opportunity surfaces a category-specific buying anxiety in real-person language (e.g. someone naming the cost of getting the decision wrong, the difficulty of choosing between hybrid and laminate, anxiety about installer trust) — not a generic "people want flooring" framing.
- Audience moments quoted or close-paraphrased from observable VOC sources (community discussion threads, review platforms, video comment threads, search-intent surfaces), with the source type cited per opportunity.
- Gap clearly named for every opportunity — not just "audiences are talking about X" but "audiences are talking about X and the content and advertising landscape is responding badly or not at all".
- Deployment paths differentiate across format type — long-form content for high-anxiety decision moments, short-form video or paid creative for quick frustrations, comparison or trust-building content for the choice-paralysis space.
- Buyer-language extract appended at the end, organised by emotional theme (e.g. *fear of expensive mistake*, *confusion between materials*, *trust in installer*), with at least five to eight tagged phrases.
- Confidence noted per opportunity, with at least one of the opportunities marked medium or low and the reason given.
- Australian market context applied where signal differs from US or global discourse on the category.

## What bad looks like

- Opportunities framed in category or marketing language ("consumers seek flooring that balances durability and aesthetics") rather than the audience's own words
- A list of trends or themes presented as if they were opportunities, without a gap named or a deployment path attached
- Generic SEO-style topics ("flooring trends 2026", "best flooring for living rooms") presented as opportunities
- Every opportunity given the same confidence rating, or no confidence rating at all
- Buyer-language extract missing, or composed of paraphrased marketing voice rather than direct quotes
- US-based VOC sources used as if they were Australian signal without flagging the geographic mismatch
- Saturated topics (already-well-covered by competitors and existing content) presented as opportunities without naming the saturation

## Notes

This eval tests the gap-naming reflex on a fully-specified brand brief. The failure mode this catches: producing an opportunity-shaped document that is actually a topic list, where every entry has audience signal but no evidence the topic is under-served. A strong answer names both sides of every gap.
