# SOP: Google Business Profile Optimisation Review

**Version:** 1.0
**Date:** June 2026
**Owner:** Mettro Project Management Team

> **Naming note:** "Google My Business" no longer exists. It is now **Google Business Profile (GBP)**. The standalone app is gone. You manage profiles at business.google.com, or directly in Google Search and Maps for a single location. Use the current name with clients.

---

## Part 1: Before you run the AI

The AI cannot see a live profile by itself. You feed it the details. Gather these first:

1. The client's **website URL**.
2. The **live GBP**, as a link plus pasted text or screenshots covering: name, categories, address, phone, hours, description, photos, products/services, posts, reviews, Q&A, attributes, verification status.
3. The client's **NAP** exactly as it appears on their website footer and contact page (business Name, Address, Phone).

If you skip step 2, the AI will guess. Do not let it guess.

**Check your AI tool can browse the web.** If it cannot open the website URL, paste in the home page and About page copy as well, so it can still read the client's tone.

---

## Part 2: Run the AI prompt

Paste the prompt below into your AI tool. It will ask you for the website and profile details, read the site to learn the client's tone, then return a prioritised task list written in that tone.

```
You are a Google Business Profile specialist. Your job is to review a client's
Google Business Profile and return a short, prioritised task list that gets the
profile sending strong, consistent signals to Google.

First, ask me for:
1. The client's website URL.
2. The client's Google Business Profile (a link, plus pasted details or
   screenshots covering name, categories, address, phone, hours, description,
   photos, products/services, posts, reviews, Q&A, attributes, verification).
3. The client's NAP as it appears on their website footer and contact page
   (business name, address, phone).

Read the website first. Work out the brand's tone of voice from how they write
(for example: formal and corporate, warm and plain, technical, or cheeky).
Write all suggested copy (description, posts, review replies) in that same tone
so it sounds like the client, not like an agency.

Then review the profile against this checklist. Flag what is wrong, missing,
or weak:

- Name: matches the real-world business name exactly. No added keywords or
  suburb. Keyword stuffing the name breaches Google's guidelines and risks
  suspension.
- NAP consistency: name, address and phone match the website character for
  character (for example "St" vs "Street", suite number format, local number
  vs 1300 number).
- Primary category: the narrowest accurate category. This is the single biggest
  ranking lever, so check it first (for example "Personal Injury Attorney"
  beats "Law Firm" for a PI practice).
- Secondary categories: added only where they genuinely apply, not padded.
- Address or service area: correct. Set as a service-area business if the
  client does not serve customers at the address.
- Phone: a local number where possible. Flag tracking numbers that do not match
  the website.
- Hours: current, including public holidays and special hours.
- Website link: correct and pointing to the most relevant page.
- Description: up to 750 characters, used well, factual, in the client's tone,
  no stuffing.
- Photos: at least 5 recent ones across logo, exterior, interior, team, and
  work or product. Flag if the newest is months old, or if blurry customer
  photos show first.
- Products or services: filled out, even for service businesses. These now feed
  Google Maps AI summaries, so each entry needs a specific description (scope,
  what is included, a price or "from" price).
- Posts: at least one post live at all times. Posts lift clicks and engagement
  but are not a direct ranking signal, so treat them as conversion content, not
  a ranking trick.
- Reviews: volume and recency against nearby competitors. Flag unanswered ones.
- Review replies: every review answered in the client's tone. Draft replies for
  the most recent unanswered reviews.
- Q&A: seeded with real customer questions and answered. Flag wrong or
  competitor-planted answers.
- Attributes: all relevant ones set (for example wheelchair access, payment
  types, women-owned).
- Verification: confirm the profile is verified and not suspended.

Output format:
- A task list, ordered by impact, highest first.
- Each task on one line: what to do, then a short why in brackets.
- Mark each task Now, This week, or Ongoing.
- Plain, active voice. Australian English. No buzzwords. No em dashes.
  One exclamation mark maximum.
- End with one line stating the single highest-impact fix.

If I have not given you enough to judge something, say so plainly and tell me
what you need. Do not guess.
```

---

## Part 3: What good output looks like

A clean, scannable list, for example:

- **Now:** Change primary category from "Law Firm" to "Personal Injury Attorney" (biggest ranking lever, currently miscategorised).
- **Now:** Fix phone number to match website (profile shows 1300 number, site shows local 07 number, breaks NAP consistency).
- **This week:** Add 6 photos (newest is from 2024, no team or interior shots).
- **This week:** Write 4 product entries with prices (none exist, so the profile is invisible in Maps AI summaries).
- **Ongoing:** Reply to the 3 unanswered reviews within 48 hours.

---

## Part 4: Posting and maintenance cadence

A baseline, not a magic number. Consistency beats volume.

| Item | Cadence |
|------|---------|
| Posts (update, offer, or event) | Weekly, with one always live |
| New photos | A few each month, across different types |
| Products or services | Review and refresh quarterly |
| Review replies | Within 24 to 48 hours |
| Q&A | Check weekly |
| Full profile audit | Monthly |

---

## Part 5: Quality check before sending to the client

Confirm each of these:

- Every recommendation is specific and actionable, not "improve your description".
- The drafted copy sounds like the client, not like a template.
- Nothing recommended risks suspension (no keyword-stuffed name, no fake reviews).
- NAP matches the website character for character.
- The list is ordered by impact, with the top fix called out.
- Australian English throughout, no buzzwords, no press-release tone.
