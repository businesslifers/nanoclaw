# DTET Workshop 1 — Run Sheet
**Taxonomy, Navigation & Information Flow**
90 mins (compresses to 60 if needed — see timing notes)

---

## Before you start

Confirm in the room: who's in the session, who has final say on decisions, and whether Sharon (IT) is present for the tech segment.

---

## Section 1 — Purpose (5 mins)

Set expectations immediately. This is a decisions session, not a discussion session.

> "Today we're not designing the site. We're agreeing on how information should be organised — so when we go into design and build, everyone is aligned. We'll make a handful of specific decisions and leave with a clear structure to work from."

Key principle to state out loud:
> "The site needs to work for people who may not be confident online. Information should be easy to find, clearly labelled, and not buried in jargon. And the team managing it needs to be able to keep it updated without it getting messy."

**Output:** Room is aligned on what success looks like today.

---

## Section 2 — Reference examples (10 mins)

Raels shows 1–2 examples of similar Mettro builds — private portals or resource-heavy intranet-style sites.

**Purpose:** Ground the room in what's possible. Non-technical clients make faster, better decisions when they can point at something rather than describe it from scratch.

**Facilitation tip:** Don't invite open feedback on the examples — use them to prompt specific reactions: "Does this kind of layout feel right for your team? Would your content sit in categories like this?"

**Output:** Shared visual reference. Clients can start pointing at things.

---

## Section 3 — Content types & taxonomy (25 mins)

This is the core of the session.

### Step A — What content exists? (10 mins)

Start with an open question: *"What kinds of resources or information does the portal need to hold?"*

Capture everything on a whiteboard/Miro. Expect answers like:
- Training videos
- PDF guides / toolkits
- Articles / written guidance
- Templates / tools (downloadable or interactive)
- Policies or frameworks
- FAQs

**Then consolidate** into agreed content types. For DTET, likely:
- Video
- PDF / download
- Article / guide
- Tool / template
- (Others TBC)

### Step B — What are the topics or themes? (10 mins)

Now ask: *"What subject areas or themes does this content cover?"*

These become the primary taxonomy dimensions — the filters and categories staff use to find things. Push for plain language, not department names.

Example themes might be: Leadership, Performance Management, Team Development, Wellbeing, Compliance — but let them name them.

**Goal:** 5–8 clear topic tags, not 20.

### Step C — What are the journey paths? (5 mins)

Ask: *"When a staff member logs in, are they coming with a specific goal in mind — like 'I'm a new manager' or 'I'm dealing with a performance issue'? Or are they browsing?"*

This confirms whether journey paths are a useful navigation layer or just content tags. If they are useful: what are the 3–5 journey labels? Keep them short and plain.

**Output:** Agreed content types, topic taxonomy (5–8 tags), journey paths (if any).

---

## Section 4 — Navigation & pathways (25 mins)

### Step A — Primary navigation (10 mins)

Show a blank nav bar. Ask: *"If you had 5–6 words in your top menu, what would they be?"*

Rules to reinforce:
- User language, not department language
- Short, obvious, plain English
- No more than 6 items
- Not too many levels deep

Typical structure for a staff portal like this:
- Home
- Browse by topic (resource library)
- My journey (or whatever the journey paths are called)
- Search

### Step B — How do users find things? (15 mins)

Walk through 2–3 real scenarios: *"A manager has just had a difficult performance conversation. How do they find what they need?"*

This reveals:
- Where content needs to be cross-linked
- What needs to appear in search results prominently
- Whether journey paths are doing enough work or if filtering/search carries more weight

Ask: *"What should come up on the homepage when someone logs in?"*

**Output:** Agreed primary nav, 2–3 documented user pathways, homepage priority decisions.

---

## Section 5 — Content management (10 mins)

Quick but important. Ask: *"Which parts of the site will change regularly?"*

Classify as:
- **Static** — set once, rarely touched (e.g. About, site structure)
- **Managed** — updated occasionally (e.g. frameworks, policies)
- **Dynamic** — updated often, needs a structured content type (e.g. new resources, announcements)

Also ask: *"Who on the team will be managing the content? How confident are they with a CMS?"*

This informs how we build the backend — simpler admin UI, clearer labelling, version history.

**Output:** List of dynamic content types, understanding of who's managing it.

---

## Section 6 — Tech Q&A (10 mins)

Open floor for IT/technical questions. Sharon (IT) likely has questions about SSO, hosting, access control, data sovereignty.

Topics likely to come up:
- SSO via Active Directory — Mettro handles the WP integration, DTET IT manages the AD group
- Hosting — to be confirmed, may need to fit QLD government infrastructure policies
- Access logging — audit trail built in, client covers plugin license
- Analytics — GA4 not suitable for private/government portals; access logging covers compliance needs

**Keep it answers-only.** If something needs investigation, park it and follow up in writing.

---

## Session outputs (confirm before leaving)

- Agreed content types
- Topic taxonomy (named tags)
- Journey paths (confirmed yes/no, and labels if yes)
- Primary navigation structure
- Homepage priority content
- List of dynamic content types + who manages them
- Any open tech questions parked for follow-up
