Honest answer: *tiered pricing is theoretically better, but only if your operations can support it. For a lot of small-to-mid agencies, it creates more problems than it solves.*

*The case FOR tiered:*
• You genuinely capture more revenue on high-value work — strategy and dev time is worth more and clients know it
• It's intellectually defensible — a partner-level strategy session isn't the same as a coordinator posting to socials
• Larger clients expect it; it signals sophistication

*The case AGAINST — and why agencies abandon it:*
• Every invoice becomes a negotiation: "was that hour strategy or execution?"
• Time tracking has to be airtight and role-coded — if it's not, you're guessing and clients feel it
• It creates internal weirdness — team members feel their work is "worth less"
• The admin overhead is real, especially at your team size
• You already moved away from it once, which means it likely caused friction

*The honest assessment:*
Tiered pricing rewards discipline. It works well at agencies with 20+ staff, dedicated account management, and proper project management software tracking hours by role. At Mettro's size, the complexity often eats the upside.

A single well-priced rate — say $200–$220/hr — executed cleanly is more profitable in practice than a tiered structure that generates scope disputes and billing awkwardness.

*The one exception:* if dev work (Archie, RJ) is a meaningful chunk of your billing, a separate dev rate is worth it. Clients already expect dev to be priced differently, so there's no pushback — and the rate gap is significant enough to matter.

So: flat rate, raise it, carve out a separate dev rate if dev is a real service line. That's my honest read.
