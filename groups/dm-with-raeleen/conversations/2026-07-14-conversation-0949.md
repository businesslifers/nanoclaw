**Janet**: <message to="slack-mg-17782">
*Option 1: Built-in batch sending*
When you schedule a campaign in Zoho Campaigns, there's a "Send in Batches" setting. You pick a batch size (e.g. 2,000 contacts) and a time gap between batches (e.g. every 2 hours). Zoho handles the rest automatically — it sends batch 1, waits, sends batch 2, and so on until the full list is done. Simple to set up, no workflow needed.

*Option 2: Random Split + wait times (workflow)*
This is done inside Zoho's automation builder. The rough setup:
1. Create a workflow triggered by a tag or list entry
2. Add a "Random Split" step — Zoho divides the contacts into however many groups you want (e.g. 7 groups for a 7-day drip)
3. Each branch gets a "Wait" step before the email action — Group 1 sends immediately, Group 2 waits 24 hours, Group 3 waits 48 hours, etc.

The difference from Option 1 is flexibility — you can add conditions, different email content per group, or vary the wait times. More setup but more control.

*Which makes more sense for Mark's situation:* Option 1 is probably the quickest win. If the funded/repaid emails are one-off blasts (not triggered per contact), batch sending is the straightforward fix. Option 2 suits ongoing automation where contacts trickle in over time.
</message>

**Janet**: <message to="slack-mg-17782">
Zoho lets you split into up to *25 batches*, with a minimum of *100 contacts per batch* and intervals of *15 minutes to 6 hours* between each one.

For Mark's 14,000-contact list that means the most stretched-out single campaign you can do natively is 25 batches × 560 contacts, with 6-hour gaps — so the full list sends over about 6 days.

If that's not spread out enough, the workaround is to split the list into multiple campaigns manually (e.g. 5 separate campaigns of 2,800 contacts each, scheduled on different days). More admin, but it gives you more control over pacing.

The Random Split workflow approach effectively gets around the 25-batch cap since it's not a "campaign batch" — it's an automation that drips contacts through over days or weeks. So if 25 batches isn't enough throttling, that's the better route.
